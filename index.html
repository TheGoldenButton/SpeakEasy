🎡 Emoji wheel

Press the index trigger on either controller (desktop: press L) to open the wheel — it opens in that hand and stays open; press again to close it and send. The golden ball rests in the centre. Point your other controller at it, hold its trigger (desktop: mouse button) and drag toward an emoji on the rim until the ball turns green, then let go to pick. Let go anywhere else and it returns to the centre. Closing the wheel sends your picks above your head for a few seconds; you can see it in the mirror.

🧚 Ask Spark

With the wheel closed, hold the index trigger on its own (desktop: hold L), speak your question, then let go. A quick tap still opens the wheel. A ball of light forms while you speak, then the fairy emerges and answers in emoji. It does not translate — it answers, using what you are looking at (in AR, the headset-camera view).

🌌 Ember sky text

Ask an information question — e.g. “the connection between consciousness and quantum theory” — and Muse Ember (the fairy) gives an emoji sequence above her head while projecting the relevant found text into the sky as a slow Star Wars-style crawl. Click a cyan source link in the sky to open it in a new tab. Drag the sky (mouse, touch, or a controller trigger) to move the whole text around the sky. Mouse-wheel over the sky scrolls it. The full sky text and its source links stay in 🌲 history for review.

🎙️ Translate to emoji

Hands only: with the wheel closed, pinch both index fingers together and hold. Controllers: hold index + grip together on one controller with the wheel closed (desktop: hold V), or hold the trigger of the wheel hand while the wheel is open (desktop: hold L with the wheel open). Speak, then let go to send. Wave your free hand through the transcript box (desktop: Esc) to cancel.

📷 AR / VR

Tap AR Mode at the top (or the 📷 button in-headset) to switch between the virtual room and the real world through the headset cameras, without leaving the headset. Ask Spark uses that real-world view. Room fit: while in AR the headset scans your walls, ceiling height, tables, couches, beds, doors and windows. When you leave AR the virtual room resizes to your real space with stand-ins for that furniture.

🕹️ Moving & pages

The left thumbstick (desktop: WASD or arrow keys to walk, Shift to hurry, drag the mouse to look) walks you around, even with the wheel open. In AR mode the thumbstick (and hand-gesture walking, snap-turn and jump) is switched off — you simply walk around your real home, and Spark stays inside your real walls and walks around your furniture. The right thumbstick left/right (desktop: Q/E, arrows or the mouse wheel while the wheel is open) flips wheel pages, or snap-turns you when the wheel is closed. A (right controller) / Space (desktop) jumps. B / Y (desktop: Esc) closes the wheel without sending; desktop Enter sends, Backspace or right-click removes the last emoji. Hands only: with the wheel open, pinch the middle fingers of both hands to close it without sending, and middle-pinch your free hand and swipe left/right to flip pages. Beckon with a "come here" motion to walk forward; push an open palm away to walk backward; swipe your hand left or right through the air to turn; flick both palms up to jump. Pinches are for the wheel and Spark.

⚙️ Settings & hands

In VR, point at ⚙️ at the top of your view, pull the trigger, then tap ✏️ to type your API key with the Meta keyboard (Meta Quest Browser 26.1+). With hand tracking, pinch index = trigger and pinch middle finger = grip. On desktop your right arm points wherever the mouse cursor points.

🙂 Your avatar

You are a stick man sized to your real height in the headset: start sitting and he starts small at that sitting height; stand up and he grows to your standing height at once. He only shrinks again if you squat / stay sat down well below your height for more than 3 seconds — a quick duck or bend keeps your full height. Its mouth is a line that turns into an O while you speak (microphone lip-sync; headsets with WebXR face tracking drive it directly). Arms follow your controllers or hands. Its face shows the emotion of the emoji you send — smiles, frowns, angry brows, tears, blushing, heart eyes, sunglasses and more — stepping through your message in time with the symbols, then relaxing. Sound effects can be switched off and on in settings.

Drift around the white platform as it flies through space, and check yourself out in the mirror. WASD / left stick moves, drag looks around.

Settings — Meta / Muse APIs

One Meta API key is used for both Muse Voice Transcribe and Muse Spark. It is stored only in this browser (localStorage), never in this file. With a key: speech goes to Muse Voice Transcribe, the text goes to Muse Spark. Without a key, voice falls back to the headset's built-in speech recognition (the same engine as the keyboard's mic button, started automatically with no keyboard popping up) and Spark falls back to a small local phrase translator.

Spark receives one message: for translation, a short instruction to translate what you said into an emoji sequence of 12 or less; for Ask Spark (trigger-hold, wheel closed), an instruction to actually answer your question helpfully as a separate assistant, using the scene image for context. Lewd or hateful content is not translated.

Before you enter

SpeakEasy can use cloud AI for voice and Ask Spark. If you allow it: your microphone audio is sent for transcription, the text goes to Muse Spark, and for Ask Spark a picture of what you are looking at (in AR, a headset camera frame) is attached. These go to this app's proxy server (or Meta directly if you add your own key in settings). Nothing is sent until you hold the trigger to speak.

If you stay offline, voice uses the headset's built-in speech recognition and Spark uses a small local phrase translator, and no audio or images leave this page. You can change this later in settings.
